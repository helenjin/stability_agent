## Current signals

All from the **106 matched MythBusters SciFy / ClaimSpy** examples, 3 source conditions:
`all` (web+nonweb), `nonweb`, `parametric_v0`. Sources: `reports/source_comparison_balanced.{md,json}`,
`reports/claimspy_followup_summary.md`, `reports/claimspy_gap_audit_systematic.md`.

### S1 — Parametric is markedly more stable on average (supports H1)
| source | n | avg stability | low (≤0.3) | high (≥0.8) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `all` | 106 | 0.330 | 60 | 17 |
| `nonweb` | 106 | 0.318 | 72 | 32 |
| `parametric_v0` | 106 | 0.695 | 1 | 36 |

Pairwise: parametric beats retrieved on the matched set — `all−param` mean −0.365 (73/106 param higher),
`nonweb−param` mean −0.377 (72/106 param higher). The two *retrieved* sources are near-identical
(`all−nonweb` mean +0.012). **Web vs nonweb barely matters; parametric vs retrieved is the real axis.**

### S2 — Stability ≠ correctness (supports H3, the cleanest signal)
- Correlation between stability and ClaimSpy quality score ≈ **0.03–0.06** across all three sources — essentially zero.
- Within the parametric source, stability is flat across quality buckets: high-quality 0.695 vs low-quality 0.688.
  Retrieved sources show a mild quality tilt (e.g. `nonweb` high-q 0.344 vs low-q 0.290) but it's small.
- In the gap audit's 18 parametric-stable cases, quality is a coin flip: **9 stable-correct, 7 stable-wrong, 2 mid**
  (mean q 0.546). Stable-wrong = perturbation-robust *and* low-quality reasoning → rigidity, not reliability.

### S3 — The "param≈1.0 / retrieved≈0.0" picture is the tail, not the norm
- Of 106 matched examples, **83 have a cross-source gap ≥ 0.30**; parametric is most stable in 65/83.
- But parametric's mean stability in gap cases is only **0.688** (≥0.80 in 18, 0.50–0.80 in 38, <0.50 in 9).
  The extreme 1.0-vs-0.0 cases are a minority tail.
- **Retrieval wins in 18/83 gap cases** (nonweb 12, web 6), almost always nonweb reaching ~1.0 — and some are
  high-quality. So perfect stability is *not* unique to parametric memory. These are the live counter-examples to H1.

### S4 — Dominant instability signature: retrieval oversensitivity
- `retrieval_oversensitivity` = 45/83 gap cases: parametric stable, retrieved collapses to ≤0.10 and is tagged
  `radius_insensitive_disagreement` — a single added claim flips the retrieved verdict.
- Across all runs, `radius_insensitive_disagreement` appears in **129 low-stability** examples (vs knife-edge in only 9).
  Instability is mostly broad disagreement, not a sharp threshold.
- Added-claim direction skews skeptical: retrieved verdicts shift `lower` (more refuting) in 47 vs `higher` in 31.

### S5 — Instability scales with claim count, not semantic redundancy
- Stability correlates negatively with `total_claims` (−0.319) and `avg_selected` (−0.331); radius-insensitive
  disagreement correlates positively with `total_claims` (+0.417). More addable claims → more brittle.
- **Semantic redundancy features are inert**: `semantic_redundancy_score` corr with stability ≈ −0.011; all
  pairwise redundancy/entailment/role proxies ≈ 0. Low- and high-stability groups have identical redundancy (~0.023).
  → The current semantic-redundancy hypothesis is *not* supported; instability is driven by raw evidence-set size/composition.

### S6 — Domain view (parametric uniformly more stable; no domain escapes the gap)
Parametric stability is ~0.65–0.73 across every domain while retrieved stays ~0.20–0.42.
Largest gap concentration: `computational_tools` (29 gap cases, mean gap 0.658), `alloys` (14, 0.639),
`superconductors` (10, 0.688).

## Provenance note — why `parametric_v0`?
- The reports label the parametric source `ss_rate_dicts_all_parametric_v0` verbatim from its directory name.
  `_v0` is a **run-version suffix on the saved output**, not a different perturbation method or model setting.
- There are two parametric dirs in `../mythbusters/notebooks/scify_soft_stability/`:
  `ss_rate_dicts_all_parametric` (35 files, partial) and `ss_rate_dicts_all_parametric_v0` (complete).
  The balanced comparison used `_v0` because it's the only parametric run with full coverage — a 106-example
  matched comparison is impossible against the 35-file dir. Parametric has only a v0 (no v1); the web condition has v0 and v1.
- The `_v0` dir lists 107 `.json` but has **106 examples** (`0.json`–`105.json`). The extra file is
  `original_alphas_dict.json` — the base-α mask metadata, not an example. `io.py` skips non-digit stems
  (`json_path.stem.isdigit()`), so n=106 in the reports is correct. (`.ipynb_checkpoints/` is also present and ignored.)
- The README quick-start shows the plain `...parametric` name only as a generic illustration.

## ScholarQA internal-vs-retrieved stability — 3-example pilot
Goal: ScholarQA has no parametric channel; evidence types are `local file search` (retrieved),
`reasoning`/`claim` (internal). Pilot tests "is internal more stable than retrieved" at the evidence level.
Setup: judge = OpenAI **gpt-4.1-mini** (same as SciFy), feasibility Likert −2..2 prompt; `x` = evidence
citation texts; `claim`-type entry = hypothesis; two base masks (internal-on vs retrieved-on), perturb adds
the complement; true_label = judge verdict on the base mask; eps=0.15, delta=0.2; radius 1–3.
Script: `scratchpad/scholar_stability_pilot.py` → `reports/scholar_stability_pilot.json`. Cost: 786 calls ≈ $0.15.

| example | SQA likert | internal-base (n_base, avg) | retrieved-base (n_base, avg) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| alloys_0014 | +2 | 2, 0.833 (1.0/0.885/0.615) | 3, **1.0** (1.0/1.0) |
| alloys_0011 | −2 | 4, **1.0** | 4, **1.0** |
| alloys_0007 | +1 | 1, 0.416 (0.365/0.365/0.519) | 3, **0.558** |

Early read: **retrieved-base ≥ internal-base in all 3** — directionally *opposite* to SciFy's parametric>retrieved.
But heavily caveated:
- **Base-size confound (biggest):** the condition with fewer base units has more units to add → mechanically
  lower stability. alloys_0007 internal has only 1 base unit (3 to add) vs retrieved 3 base (1 to add), so its
  low internal score partly reflects n_add, not source robustness. Need to control base size before trusting direction.
- n=3, alloys-only; not significant.
- Judge base label sometimes differs from ScholarQA's likert and between conditions (e.g. ex14 internal-base label=1
  vs retrieved-base=2; ex7 retrieved-base=0 vs SQA +1) — stability is measured against the judge's own base label, as designed.
- ScholarQA "internal" = `reasoning` only (sparse); not true closed-book parametric memory.
Next: control for base-mask size (equal n_base or normalize by n_add), expand beyond alloys, then decide on a true closed-book parametric condition.

### Base-size-controlled rerun (12 fresh examples, 7 domains)
Control: per example, k = min(n_internal, n_retrieved, 3); subsample BOTH base and add-pool to exactly k units,
so both conditions have equal base size and equal perturbation magnitude (radius 1..k) — only base-vs-add source swaps.
Script: `scratchpad/scholar_stability_controlled.py` → `reports/scholar_stability_controlled.json`.
Disk-cached judge (`scratchpad/judge_cache.json`); this run: 921 API calls + 2223 cache hits (~$0.18). Excludes the 3 pilot examples.

**n=12, mean Δ(internal−retrieved) = +0.134; internal more stable 5, retrieved more stable 2, tie 5.**
- **Direction flips vs the uncontrolled pilot** → internal/reasoning-grounded verdicts are *more* stable than
  retrieved-grounded ones, **directionally consistent with SciFy H1** (parametric/internal > retrieved). The pilot's
  "retrieved more stable" was the base-size artifact, now removed.
- 5 ties are all at 1.0/1.0 and 4/5 are likert ≤ −1 — **strongly-infeasible verdicts are saturated/robust regardless of source**;

Next: scale to ~30–40 examples for significance; add stability×correctness (does ScholarQA likert agree with the judge's base label?); then decide on a true closed-book parametric condition.

### ScholarQA source summary (SciFy-style table, natural sizes — NOT size-controlled)
Mirrors the SciFy source table. `internal`/`retrieved` = that source as base, perturb by adding the other source,
true_label = judge's verdict on the base mask (self-consistency). `all` = empty base perturbed by the full mixed pool.
Judge = gpt-4.1-mini. Scripts: `scratchpad/scholar_stability_{allsource,uncapped}.py` → `reports/scholar_stability_{allsource,uncapped}.json`.

| source | n | avg stability | low (≤0.3) | high (≥0.8) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `all` (empty base, full pool) | 67 | 0.238 | 44 | 0 |
| `retrieved` (literature base) | 62 | 0.777 | 8 | 39 |
| `internal` (reasoning base) | 62 | 0.812 | 4 | 40 |

`all` reference fix (2026-06-18): originally `all` used true_label = ScholarQA likert → 0.497, which measured
*agreement-with-ScholarQA* while internal/retrieved measured *self-consistency*. Re-ran `all` with the
self-consistent reference (judge's own empty-base verdict) → **0.238**. Only the label changed; all perturbation
verdicts were cached (684 new calls = empty-base verdicts, 17,011 cache hits). Now all three use the same reference convention.
Still NOT fully comparable: `all` is a different operating point (empty base, largest add-pool → most-perturbed by construction),
so its low score is partly mechanical, not a "source is worse" result. Read `all` as full-pool self-consistency, not a third source on equal footing.
Mapping to SciFy: `all`↔`all`; `internal` (reasoning) ↔ `parametric_v0` (internal memory); `retrieved` (local file search) ↔ `web`/`nonweb`.
Read: source-specific bases far more stable than mixed `all`; internal/reasoning base most stable (0.812; echo of SciFy parametric 0.695).
Caveats: n differs by row (67 vs 62 — `all` needs ≥1 unit, internal/retrieved need ≥1 of each type; the 62 ⊂ 67).
Internal-vs-retrieved gap is small at natural sizes (0.812 vs 0.777); widens to +0.196 only when size-controlled (see below).
