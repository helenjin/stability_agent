% --- Notes on reading all three tables together ---
%
% - Both SAGER variants continue to roughly halve TopoDev and cut TopoVar
%   ~4x relative to ARES at n=18 (same direction as n=5, now on 3.6x more
%   data): conditioning on the graph's actual parents/ancestors instead of
%   the full sequence prefix makes the raw stability *score* much less
%   sensitive to which valid ordering is shown. Parents vs. ancestors remain
%   statistically indistinguishable on this metric (heavily overlapping CIs).
%
% - On F1, both SAGER variants continue to score much higher than ARES, and
%   the gap widened as more data came in: 0.610 / 0.556 vs. 0.283 (was
%   0.503 / 0.415 vs. 0.198 at n=5). ARES's F1 CI has moved well off 0 now
%   (only 4 zero-predicted-positive orderings across all 3 methods combined
%   at n=18, down from 18 at n=5 -- driven by ares alone both times).
%
% - The n=5 anomaly is RESOLVED: at n=5, SAGER (direct parents) looked like
%   the LEAST order-stable method by Delta F1 (0.127) despite having the
%   lowest raw-score TopoDev -- flagged at the time as likely noise. At
%   n=18 that inverts as expected: SAGER (parents) Delta F1 = 0.082 and
%   SAGER (ancestors) Delta F1 = 0.073, both far more stable than ARES's
%   0.219, and close to each other. Confirms it was noise from too few
%   graphs, not a real effect -- good example of why n=5 conclusions here
%   should always be held loosely.
%
% - mean_exact_match_rate is still 0.0 for all three methods at n=18: across
%   every recipe seen so far, no method ever predicts the EXACT SAME error
%   set in all 10 orderings of a recipe. This looks like it may be a
%   structural property of the setup (K=10 orderings basically always turns
%   up at least one disagreement, whichever method) rather than a small-n
%   artifact -- worth stating plainly rather than as a method comparison
%   point once the full 24 are in.
